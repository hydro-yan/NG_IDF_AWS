Next-Generation Intensity–Duration–Frequency (NG-IDF) Hydrological Prediction Tool

Welcome to the NG-IDF Tool

The NG-IDF Tool enables hydrologists and engineers to generate both conventional precipitation-based IDF (PREC-IDF) curves, commonly used in surface water design manuals, and advanced NG-IDF curves that incorporate snowmelt and rain-on-snow (ROS) processes. This unified framework provides a consistent approach for estimating extreme storms and floods across both rainfall- and snow-dominated regions (Yan et al., 2018).

Disclaimer: This tool is a research and screening product intended to support preliminary comparisons between conventional PREC-IDF estimates and the advanced NG-IDF approach. Results are provided for informational and exploratory purposes only and are not intended for direct use in hydrologic design or facility engineering. Final engineering and regulatory approvals must be obtained through applicable governing standards and authorities.

Methods and References
Methods used in the NG-IDF Tool:
  • Historical weather: Daymet gridded meteorological data (1989/06/01–2021/09/30, 1-km grid, 24-hour resolution) (Thornton et al., 2021).
  • Future scenarios #1: Direct downscaling of four CESM2-Large Ensemble (CESM2-LE) members (30-year reference [1989/06/01–2021/09/30] and 30-year future periods [2033/06/01–2065/09/30], 4-km grid, 1-hour resolution). These simulations utilize the SSP3-70 scenario. While PGW intensifies known historical events, these direct downscaling runs capture the full range of natural weather variability, providing a robust look at how entirely new, unrecorded weather patterns might emerge in a changing future.
  • Future scenarios #2: WRF baseline forced with ERA5 (1989/06/01–2021/09/30, 4-km grid,1-hour resolution) and two pseudo-global warming (PGW) scenarios (medium and high, 2033/06/01–2065/09/30, 4 km grid, 1-hour resolution). Perturbations follow ensemble mean changes from two sets of CMIP6 GCM ensembles focused on SSP2-45 (Cheng et al., 2025). WRF simulations act as a high-definition lens that first reconstructs the 'ground truth' of historical weather with local precision and then projects how those exact events would intensify in a future, warmer weather.
  • Snow model: Calibrated DHSVM snow model (Sun et al., 2019, Sun et al., 2022).Snow parameters have been further tuned specifically for the Alask to enhance predictive accuracy, particularly in Southern Alaska where winter temperatures frequently fluctuate near the freezing point.
  • Monthly LAI data: Global Land Surface Satellite (GLASS) leaf area index (LAI) products (Li and Xiao, 2020).
  • Vegetation height data: Landfire Existing Vegetation Height products (EVH).
  • IDF frequency analysis: Type I Generalized Extreme Value distribution using L-moments (Yan et al., 2026).
  • IDF uncertainty: Monte Carlo ensemble (Yan et al., 2020).
