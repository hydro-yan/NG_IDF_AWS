Intensified Historical Storms Results: This page asks a single question: if the storms that actually happened here were to occur in a warmer weather, how much bigger would they be? The WRF model re-runs the historical weather with warming added, so the baseline and future panels contain the same storms under different weather conditions. .

How to use these outputs:
  • PREC-IDF reports precipitation-only design-storm depth for each duration and average recurrence interval (ARI).
  • NG-IDF reports water available for runoff, incorporating cold-region processes such as snowmelt and rain-on-snow in addition to rainfall.
  • Impact Summary compares the Moderate (+1.72°C) and Severe (+2.36°C) scenarios NG-IDF values with the historical baseline (difference in inches and percent change).
  • Annual-maximum tables (in the Detailed Data Tables) list the largest event in each water year. The event mechanism identifies whether the annual runoff maximum was driven by rainfall (R), snowmelt (M), or rain-on-snow (ROS).
  • The reported 90% confidence intervals describe uncertainty in the fitted IDF estimates and can support risk-informed design decisions.

Use the change, not the absolute values. The baseline simulation is still a model result, so the change from the historical baseline to the warmer scenario is the transferable result. Apply that change to the observed values on the Historical Weather (Daymet) page:

  • IDF — percent change (e.g., +20%) → Multiply: if the Historical Weather (Daymet) NG-IDF value is 2.0 in, then 2.0 in × (1 + 20%) = 2.4 in. For example, if the Moderate (+1.72°C) scenario NG-IDF is 20% higher than the historical baseline NG-IDF, multiply the Daymet NG-IDF value by 1.20.

Two stress tests. Moderate (+1.72°C) and Severe (+2.36°C) apply different amounts of warming. Neither is more likely; they bracket a plausible range. Use Moderate as a central estimate and check Severe for anything long-lived or costly to fail.
