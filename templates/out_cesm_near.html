Near-Term Sensitivity Stress Test Results: CESM (Community Earth System Model) generates new 2034–2049 weather from physics. The baseline simulation (2006–2021) is the model's version of the past, shown only as the reference that the future runs are measured against. All displayed design values are reported in inches.

How to use these outputs:
  • PREC-IDF reports precipitation-only design-storm depth for each duration and average recurrence interval (ARI).
  • NG-IDF reports water available for runoff, incorporating cold-region processes such as snowmelt and rain-on-snow in addition to rainfall.
  • Impact Summary compares the future NG-IDF with the baseline for the ensemble mean (first column) and for each member (difference in inches and percent change).
  • Annual-maximum tables (in the Detailed Data Tables) list the largest event in each water year. The event mechanism identifies whether the annual runoff maximum was driven by rainfall (R), snowmelt (M), or rain-on-snow (ROS).
  • The reported 90% confidence intervals describe uncertainty in the fitted IDF estimates and can support risk-informed design decisions.

Use the change, not the absolute values. The baseline is still a model simulation, so the change from baseline to 2034–2049 is the transferable result. Apply it to the observed values on the Historical Weather (Daymet) page:

  • IDF — percent change (e.g., +20%) → Multiply: if the Historical Weather (Daymet) NG-IDF value is 2.0 in, then 2.0 in × (1 + 20%) = 2.4 in. For example, if a member's future NG-IDF is 20% higher than its baseline NG-IDF, multiply the Daymet NG-IDF value by 1.20.

Use the ensemble-mean change (the first column of the Impact Summary, computed from the ensemble-mean baseline and the ensemble-mean future NG-IDF) as the central estimate, and use the spread across members (the shaded bands in the Ensemble Mean figures, or the member columns in the Impact Summary) to see how much it could vary. For risk-averse designs, consider the maximum (or minimum) member change instead, depending on your risk-cost tradeoff. Each future member is labelled with its own domain-mean air-temperature change (ΔTair).
